High-Yield NCERT Revision Notes

Class 12 Chapter 1: Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

1. Microsporangium & Pollen Grain Anatomy

Wall Layers (Outermost to Innermost)

1. Epidermis: Outer single protective layer.
2. Endothecium: Hygroscopic fibrous thickenings of alpha-cellulosic bands aiding anther dehiscence.
3. Middle Layers: 1–3 ephemeral protective layers.
4. Tapetum: Innermost nutritive layer, multinucleate with dense cytoplasm, synthesizes callase, enzymes, and Ubisch bodies/sporopollenin.

Pollen Grain Structure & Shedding

• Exine: Made of sporopollenin, one of the most resistant organic substances known. Has germ pores where sporopollenin is absent.
• Intine: Thin, continuous inner layer made of cellulose and pectin.
• In >60% of angiosperms, pollen is shed at the 2-celled stage (Vegetative cell + Generative cell). In the remaining <40%, the generative cell divides mitotically to form 2 male gametes (3-celled stage).

2. Megasporangium (Ovule) & Embryo Sac

Anatomy of an Anatropous Ovule

• Funicle: Stalk attaching the ovule to the placenta.
• Hilum: Scar representing the point of attachment between funicle and body of the ovule.
• Micropyle: Small aperture where integuments do not cover the nucellus.
• Chalaza: Basal region opposite the micropyle.
• Nucellus: Central vegetative nutritive tissue ($2n$). Persistent nucellus in seeds is called perisperm (e.g. black pepper, beet).

7-Celled, 8-Nucleate Polygonum Embryo Sac

Develops from 1 functional megaspore via 1 Meiosis + 3 Mitoses (monosporic development):
• Micropylar Egg Apparatus (3 cells): 1 Egg cell + 2 Synergids with filiform apparatus that guides pollen tube entry.
• Central Cell (1 large cell): Contains 2 polar nuclei ($n + n$).
• Chalazal Antipodals (3 cells): Function primarily in nutrient conduction.

3. Pollination Types, Contrivances & Double Fertilization

Types of Pollination

• Autogamy: Same flower.
• Geitonogamy: Different flowers on the same plant (functionally cross-pollination, genetically autogamy).
• Xenogamy: Different flowers on different plants (brings genetically different pollen to stigma).

Outbreeding Devices

• Dichogamy: Non-synchronous pollen release and stigma receptivity.
• Herkogamy: Spatial separation of anther and stigma.
• Self-incompatibility: Genetically controlled inhibition of pollen germination.
• Dioecy: Male and female flowers on separate plants (prevents both autogamy and geitonogamy, e.g. papaya).

Double Fertilization

• Syngamy: 1 Male gamete ($n$) + Egg cell ($n$) $ ightarrow$ Zygote ($2n$).
• Triple Fusion: 1 Male gamete ($n$) + 2 Polar nuclei ($2n$) $ ightarrow$ Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, $3n$).
• Unique to angiosperms; endosperm development precedes embryo development to ensure nutrient availability.
